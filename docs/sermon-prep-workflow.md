# ADHD - Bryan Schneider - Christ Centered Sermon Prep

This is the canonical workflow Bryan uses for sermon prep. Originally built in Logos as a Workflow document; ported into this companion's study module. **Every LLM, tool, and UI surface in this project must respect this structure.** Where the live code or any new design diverges from this document, the document wins unless Bryan explicitly approves a change.

The workflow has **16 numbered phases** (some with sub-phases). Each phase has its own timer budget so an ADHD-prone preacher can stay on track without rabbit-holing.

**Hard rule the LLM must respect (see `docs/llm-role-in-sermon-prep.md`):** the LLM is a **research graduate assistant**, not an author. It surfaces material on demand, discusses, and reviews manuscript. It does NOT write sermons or produce sermon content.

---

## Phase 1 — Pray (15 min)

> *Extremely Important! Pray and ask God for help. Especially with your easily distracted mind.*

**LLM role:** silent. Bryan's heart preparation. Not the time for an AI presence at all.

---

## Phase 2 — Translate the Text (2 hours)

Bryan's own translation work. Questions to wrestle with:

- What would your own translation be?
- Were there any really odd words?
- Was there repetition?
- Were there any references to other texts?

**Logos resource pulled in:** Cross References (Treasury of Scripture Knowledge — the references list shown in the screenshot).

**LLM role:** silent unless explicitly invited. Bryan is wrestling with the original language himself. If invited, the LLM can pull lexicon entries or surface cross-references from his library, but never propose a translation.

---

## Phase 3 — Digest (1 hour)

> *Write out a prayer for each phrase, sentence, or paragraph. Make this a time for spiritual worship.*

**LLM role:** silent. This is devotion, not exegesis.

---

## Phase 4 — Exegesis (1 hour)

Heaviest exegetical phase. Multiple Logos resources auto-pull in. Questions and tool panels:

- **Textual Variants** — Textual Commentaries (Metzger 2nd ed., A Textual Guide to GNT, Introduction to NT in Original Greek: Appendix), Apparatuses (NA, SBL, etc.), Editions, Transcriptions, Ancient Versions, Online Manuscripts. Question: *Are there any significant textual variants?*
- **Figurative Language** — categories like "Righteousness of God as The Act…", "Blood as Death", "Conduit as Faith", "Instrument as Blood", etc. Question: *Is there any figurative language?*
- **Literary Typing** — e.g., Romans → Letter → Community → Body → Expositional Argument. Question: *What literary typing is there?*
- **Outlines** — published outlines from The Bible Guide, The Bible Knowledge Commentary, Bible Study Outlines, The Companion Bible, A Critical and Exegetical Commentary on Romans, CSB Study Bible Notes.
- **Sentence diagram** — "It is helpful to follow the sentence digram to see what the grammar says the subject and main verb is. This is especially important in epistles." Question: *What is the subject grammatically?*
- **Section/book questions** — *What's going on in this section of the book?* with prompts: *Who is ___? What is the reader expected to know about them? What does ___ mean in this context? Why is this detail included at this point? What purpose does ___ serve in the narrative? Why did the character say or do ___? Was this expected/acceptable behavior at the time? What might be the original audience's visceral reaction to this? How does it fit with ___ [another part of the CUT, book, or Bible]?*
- **Grammatical Constructions** — Phrase Level Connection, Prepositional Phrases by particle (εἰς, διά, πρός, ἐν, ἐκ) with object case (Accusative, Genitive, Dative) and object lemma. Question: *What grammatical constructions are being used? Are they significant?*

**LLM role:** research grad assistant in research mode. When invited, fetches lexicon/grammar/textual-variant info from Bryan's library and returns sources + quotes + citations. May discuss findings if Bryan opens a conversation. Does not propose what the grammar means — Bryan reads the sources.

---

## Phase 5 — Get Quick Answers (30 min) — Study Bibles

> *Study Bibles can get you on the right track quickly. You are preaching and not writing an entire in depth exegesis paper. You only have a limited number of hours to get this task accomplished.*
> *Remember time is of the essence.*
> *Don't chase every rabbit just because it interests you.*
> *You only budget 30 minute for this. Start a timer.*
> *Open the custom guide - 'Study Bible Collection'*

Question: *Is there anything significant you were missing?*

**LLM role:** silent unless invited. If invited, may surface notes from the 7 study bibles already integrated. The phase is intentionally TIME-LIMITED so the LLM should not encourage going long.

---

## Phase 6 — Context Questions (15 min)

- *What is the temporal setting?*
- *What is the geographic setting?* + Atlas resource
- *Are there cultural things that color this? Get the picture in your mind. This is not a rabbit trail but trying to get the lived experience of the passage.*
- Resource panels: Cultural Concepts, Biblical Places, Biblical Things (e.g., Sacrifice, Blood — with associated images), Media Resources, Media Collections.

**LLM role:** silent unless invited. If invited, pulls relevant cultural/geographic background from the library with citations.

---

## Phase 7 — Big Picture (30 min)

- *What is the point of this passage in the larger picture of this book?*
- *Are there surrounding units of thought that should be kept in mind?*
- *Where does this story fit in within the canonical and historical redemptive context?*

**LLM role:** discussion partner when invited. Can banter on the place of the passage in the canon, but again from sources Bryan can verify.

---

## Phase 8 — Identify Christ (30 min)

> *This is especially important in OT passages. However, even in epistles Jesus must be preached even in the midst of imperative sections like the end of Ephesians.*

Seven lenses (the prompt says: "Use these 7 lenses — not all will apply to every text"):

1. **Historical Redemptive progress** — bottom (personal), middle (national), top (receptive). What does the passage reveal about God and His will? About the whole Bible? In light of Christ? About Christ?
2. **Promise-fulfillment** — Is there a messianic hope?
3. **Typology** — Is the historical (person, action, event, institution)? Is the central message about God's redemptive activity? Does the type have symbolic meaning (else it's not a type)? Is it used by God in dealing with Israel? Is there a significant analogy with antitype? Escalation from type to antitype? Points of contrast between OT type and NT antitype? Meaning of the symbol that escalates to Christ (Greidanus 259)? **Preach Christ — not the significance of the type but Christ himself.**
4. **Analogy** — Parallel situation with the NT? What was God doing for Israel and what IS God in Christ doing for the church?
5. **Longitudinal themes** — What truth about God and his saving work is disclosed? How is it carried forward in revelation? How does it find fulfillment in Christ? Are any of the "highways" present? (KOG, providence, covenant, presence of God, love of God, grace of God, justice, redemption, law, sin & guilt offerings, God's concern for the poor, mediator, the Day of the Lord, etc.)
6. **Contrast** — A change from the OT to the new in Christ.
7. **NT Reference** — Is this passage referenced in the New Testament, giving a new hermeneutical lens?

**LLM role:** Bryan's own thinking work. If invited, the LLM can ask which lens he sees operating and let him answer — Socratic. May pull supporting sources (Greidanus 259 etc.) on request. Must NOT propose Christ-thread angles unsolicited.

---

## Phase 9 — Systematic & Biblical Studies Issues (30 min)

- *Are there any systematic issues that need to be examined or explained?* — Systematic Theologies panel (Theology Proper, Christology, Soteriology, Anthropology, Demonology, Hamartiology, Ecclesiology, Theologians)
- *Are there any Biblical theology issues that need to be examined or explained?* — Biblical Theologies panel (Foundations, God, Jesus Christ)

**LLM role:** research mode when invited. Surfaces relevant sections from systematic/biblical theology resources with citations.

---

## Phase 10 — Don't live in a historical vacuum (30 min)

> *Are there any significant writings from the church fathers that change how I have understood this passage?*

Ancient Literature panel: Apostolic Fathers, Church Fathers (Basil, Origen, Augustine, Epiphanius, Cyril of Alexandria, Hilary, Jerome, Chrysostom…), Dead Sea Scrolls Sectarian Material, Judaica (Berakhot, Sheqalim, Yoma, Taanit, Sotah, Makkot, Horayot, Shabbat, Sukkot, Megilla, Hagiga, Sanhedrin…), Old Testament Pseudepigrapha (1 Enoch, Odes of Solomon…), Works of Josephus, Works of Philo.

**LLM role:** research grad assistant in its purest form. When invited, scours the patristic + ancient corpus, brings back relevant quotes with citation. Does NOT synthesize "what the Fathers say" — surfaces the texts themselves.

---

## Phase 11 — Smarter people than you have been here — Confessions (15 min)

> *Are there any confessional documents that reference this passage?*

Confessional Documents panel: Theology Proper, Christology, Soteriology, Traditions.

**LLM role:** research mode. Surface confessional references.

---

## Phase 12 — Questions still left? (30 min) — Commentaries

> *Okay now you can turn to commentaries. Don't get bogged down in this too quickly.*
> *You know the temptation to spend all week reading commentaries and waiting until Sunday morning to write your sermon. Don't do that.*
> *Just find what you need. You haven't even started writing the sermon yet.*

Commentaries panel: Matthew Henry, Metzger Textual Commentary, UBSH, NIVZNDR Biblical Theology Study Bible, FSB, NICNT, WBC, ICC, PNTC, etc., sortable by Priority / Series / Author / Denomination / Type / Era.

**LLM role:** strict research mode with discipline. The phase is explicitly time-boxed against the temptation to drown in commentaries. The LLM should respect that — when invited, find ONLY what Bryan asks for from the named commentaries he picks, and stop. Do not volunteer additional commentary opinions.

## Phase 13 — Big Picture - Sermon Work

Now the LLM transition: Bryan moves from research into sermon construction. The work is still HIS, but this is where the LLM can act as a discussion partner and manuscript reviewer (see role doc for the rules).

### 13.1 Determine the Subject (15 min)

> *What question is this CUT (complete unit of thought - not a single word) designed to answer about God - his nature or covenant keeping - to ancient Israel?*

- What is the 1 thing this passage is about?
- What is the unifying center?
- What aspect of God's character is being pointed out?

### 13.2 Develop the Theme — compliment (15 min)

> *what is this passage saying about the subject?*
> *What's this writer saying about what he's talking about? If the subject is a question, then the complement is the answer to that question, and the two together become the idea.*

### 13.3 Set forth Exegetical Point (EP) / Original Theological Message (OTM) (15 min)

> *To truly preach expository sermons the original meaning and intent of the author must be preserved.*

What was God saying through his Spirit through the author to the recipients of that time?

- What is this text about? (subject)
- What does the text say about it? (compliment)
- What theme holds the entire message together?
- Is the proposed EP a simple or complex sentence? (keep narrowing until simple)
- Is the EP a propositional sentence?
- Does the EP relate every sentence to the theme?

### 13.4 Establish a Transition Sentence (15 min)

> *What in the text supports the EP — example: In what ways is gospel being advanced?*

### 13.5 Develop Exegetical Outline (15 min)

> *Outline the Text Exegetically (what is the internal logical flow)?*

- Identify textual support for EP
- ask: what is being said about the EP?
- does every point support the EP?
- how does this point progress the argument of the EP?
- how does this point relate to the point before and after it?

### 13.6 Identify the Fallen Condition Focus — FCF (15 min)

> *This begins the process of moving the exegetical study to a sermon. A lecture describes the text. A sermon moves it from this is what God said to them into the realm of, "This is God's direction for you today." Spend time wrestling here in prayer. Think about the people in the congregation you'll be preaching to. What at the core of their person and experience is shared between the original audience and your hearers?*

- What does the text say?
- What spiritual concern(s) did the text address (in its context)?
- What spiritual concerns do listeners share in common with those to (or about) whom the text was written?
- What is the depravity factor? What in humanity rebels against the text's vision of God?

### 13.7 Set forth Homiletical Point (HP) / Take Home Truth (THT) (15 min)

> *A professor lectures. A pastor heralds God's word to His people today. The people of God should walk away with a sense that God's word is for them not just people who died 2000 years ago.*

> *Pray that God would give you a love for his people, insight into the word, and wisdom in explaining and applying God's word to His people's lives.*

> *Did you actually pray? No, don't skip this part. Actually go up and pray for reverence, wisdom, and love. Imagine the people in the pews. See their faces.*

What is God through me saying to his people today? What is the burden of the text? This will be the take home truth.

Now that you have your HP/THT — ask these questions:

- Is that based on the EP?
- Is it written in the 2nd person or to an anonymous "someone"?
- Is it sharp? Will it stick? Does it get under the skin?
  - Should be drawing interest into the sermon
  - HP must be concise and memorable — Will people remember the point on Wednesday?

Now we'll final edit the THT:

- HP — must have an eye toward the Fallen Condition Focus (FCF)
- Take time-specific language of EP/OTM and replace with contemporary equivalent.

> *Here is one example from a book on moving an Exegetical Point (Original Theological Message) of a narrative passage to a Take Home Truth (Homiletical Point). Remember, our calling is to preach Christ and Him crucified. A christless sermon can live in a synagogue but ought to never among the blood bought sheep of Jesus.*
>
> **OTM:** The Lord will use repentant kings of Israel to fulfill the Davidic Covenant, but despising the Lord's word brings discipline.
> **THT:** God will use us to fulfill his plan of salvation, but sin brings discipline.***
> *This should be amended to read Christologically*
> God used his King to fulfill the plan of salvation, and disciplined his son for us.***

### 13.8 Purpose Statement — Main Application (15 min)

> *This is about the overall application of the sermon. There will be lots of smaller applications but what is the main imperative of the sermon?*
>
> *There is a divine imperative in preaching. Christian preaching extols Christ and sends Christians out to do.*

- What should happen in the listeners as a result of hearing the sermon?
- What did the writer expect to accomplish by putting this story here and telling it this way?
- What do I expect to happen in the lives of the congregation after listening? (the 2 should point to the same goal)
  - Some applications are of faith (believe, receive, trust). Some are cognitive (understand, take to heart). Other times they are physical (go, flee, feed, wash). Others are spiritual (repent, hate, love, enjoy).
- Is there a measurable goal?
- What does God require of the hearers of this passage?
- Where does God require it of me?
- Why must I do what God requires of me?
- How can I do what God requires of me?

## Phase 14 — Formal Sermon Writing

### 14.1 Develop a broad preaching outline based on the exegetical outline (30 min)

> *Think of the preaching outline as a path you are wanting to take people. There is a main proposition and you are trying to bring people along on a path to get to the homiletical conclusion. Each point should solidify the truth of the HP or Take Home Truth.*

Each point of the sermon can have each of these (but not necessarily all of them). Amplifying the points of your homiletical outline will become the body of your sermon:

- Orientation
- Explanation
- Objections
- Illustration
- Application

### 14.2 Amplify the main divisions (2 hours)

The heaviest sermon-construction phase. Each amplification has its own framework:

**Orientation** — *People need to know where you are in the text, where you are in the sermon, where you are logically, where you are geographically, where you are in time.*

**Explanation** — Prove psychologically & emotionally.
- List Questions: What things wouldn't make sense to a modern audience? Geography, Customs, Names, History, Theology, Language. Any other questions people might have from the text?
- Look at Scripture Index for reference to heads of doctrines.

**Objections & defense**
- validate the point against any emotional or intellectual doubts
- Answer the objection — Is this true? Can I buy this?
- *Don't be afraid to step into these areas. This is exactly where the Holy Spirit can work in the hardest of hearts.*

**Illustrations**
- Frame the picture
  - isolate the event & associate it with a concept
  - familiar emotions, identifiable dilemmas, personal traits, situational parallels
  - see the cannons flash, feel the storm waves, fear the shoals — **illustrations should be felt**
- Does it have the parts of a story?
  - Intro, descriptive details, movement (actual or emotional), crisis, & conclusion
- ***Warning*** *Illustrations illuminate a truth. Do NOT let the sermon be nothing but illustrations. Slip into an illustration — shed the light — and get out — don't get stuck in this mode.*

**Application** — build around vision of God and depravity factor (FCF)
- What does this story teach me about God and his relationship with human beings?
- (needs, hurt, comfort, issues, trends, work/home/school)
- so what?
- what will this truth look like when it is fleshed out in their lives?
- what is God calling His people to do through this story?
- don't moralize — get at the why of the human heart

**Application Prods** — eleven categories of probing questions to test the application:

1. **Why It Matters** — What if this truth was not real? What would the world be like? What would life be like? Why do you think people need this truth? How would you explain to someone that this truth is essential to finding true joy in this world?
2. **Dissecting the Message** — Consider each word of the message. What does each word contribute? Why is each word important for what God is communicating to us here? What words or phrases would most significantly impact what this message is communicating if eliminated or altered? What are the three most important words in your stated message? If you had three minutes to tell this truth to a stranger, what part would you emphasize most, how would you do it in tone and explanation? What word or phrase could be easily glossed over but is in fact quite significant?
3. **Clarifying the Message** — How could someone misunderstand what this message is saying? How could someone make this truth say too much or too little? What might over-applying or under-applying look like? What do you think people in general find most confusing? What do you think Christians specifically find most confusing? In what ways do you think this truth is misunderstood (e.g., overheard, underheard, poorly defined)?
4. **Analyzing Our Reaction** — How do you find yourself responding to the message? What does our response teach us about ourselves and our own need for this passage? What do you find most encouraging? Most challenging? And why?
5. **Addressing Our Struggle** — Why do we fail to live in the good of the message? What are the main reasons people doubt or reject this message? What's a helpful response to each challenge? When are the moments I need this message the most? What reasons make us find it functionally difficult to live as if this is true?
6. **Challenging Frameworks** — How does the message challenge any operating frameworks we have? How does this truth challenge common cultural conceptions/viewpoints/visions of reality? Does our culture tell us a different, competing message? How might someone living in a different culture hear this with a different nuance or focus? What might we learn from their perspective? What is one way you think God may want to adjust your thinking by revealing this truth to you? What false beliefs can this truth combat?
7. **Shaping Loyalties** — How might this truth strengthen your faith in God? How might this passage help you to persevere in, or alter, a goal you have in your life?
8. **Envisioning Its Realization** — How might life be different next week if trusted in this truth more? Envision a church characterized by this message — what would it look like? What good fruit might this truth stir up when a local church collectively enjoys this truth together? What is one risk you would take if you trusted in this truth more?
9. **Ministering Its Truth** — How can you pray the truth of this passage for someone in the church? What is one way you could encourage someone with this truth? What might it look like to minister this truth to people in different situations (depressed, lonely, scared, overwhelmed, angry, hurt, arrogant, domineering, selfish, grumbling)? How might you pray this truth for this local church or for our city?
10. **In Real Life** — What are three real-life situations where this truth could give our souls stability and hope? How can this truth help you faithfully follow the Lord in particular situations this week? If someone watched your life and could hear all your thoughts, in what ways would they discover you not to be fully resting in and living out this truth?
11. **Pursuing Growth** — What is something you can do to help you grow in trusting in this truth? What motivations does this passage present for fulfilling its applications? In light of this, what might it look like to pursue these applications? What part of this truth do you give the least amount of attention to? Why? What might it look like to grow in thinking on that aspect and/or living in the good of it?
12. **Reaching Unbelievers** — How might you illustrate this truth to an unbeliever? What questions do you think this message would raise for someone who has never heard the gospel before?

**Transition** — *Try to think through how your homiletical points logically tie together. Transitions are often done well when they show how they (1) Tie into the HP & FCF, (2) Summarize the previous point, (3) Introduce the next point smoothly.*

### 14.3 Create introduction with HP/THT lead-in (15 min)

Types of introductions (pick one per sermon):

- **Front Porch** — warm welcoming, come & talk
- **Challenge / White Glove** — impossible situation; how can we evangelize; hard statistic; challenge to prayer and fasting; "I know this is going to be hard for you"
- **Mystery** — veil assets of truth to unfold as sermon goes on; "Jesus was our priest → unfold hows in points"
- **Examination** — examined before the light of God's word; before communion; prayer — point of examination is to fill holes; "say it right up front that this is what we're doing"
- **Paradox** — God's sovereignty and evangelism; "good paradoxes don't confuse or go against each other but preaching pushes people with more confidence"; God's love and wrath; sola gratia and antinomianism; "Joseph — you meant it for evil but God meant it for good"; "some of you are in horrible positions, we've been praying for you, weeping for you, how do we think about this?"
- **Occasion** — holidays, culture, elections, national crisis, death, marriages, sacraments
- **Quote** — make sure it's powerful and directed
- **Question** — you're interested in what they're talking about
- **Short Story** — children draw a picture of the story
- **Ramrod** — state HP; "Here's what's on my heart today"; just jump in, both feet; "wake up"; "what's really on my heart today is…"

**Give Points of Sermon** — *MUST have FCF*

### 14.4 Finish conclusion with repeat of THT/HP (15 min)

> *Land the plane. Tell them what they need to walk home with. Make it memorable.*

---

## Phase 15 — Finishing Questions to ask yourself (30 min)

- Have I been faithful to the text?
- Have I prayed for the different age groups?
- Am I hitting the head but missing the heart?
- Have I appealed to emotions but neglected the intellect?
- Is this a lecture or a sermon?
- Are the people going to be spurred on toward loving God and His glory?
- Have I read this missiologically?
- Have I given God's people tangible ways to apply His word this week?
- Have I bound up the wounded?
- Have I preached freedom to the captives?
- Have I brought good news to the poor?
- Does the intro have a fallen condition focus?
- Have I landed the sermon — is it tied up so the people will remember what it was about?

---

## Phase 16 — No you're not done. It's too long and has too much junk. Don't get lazy here. (45 min)

> *1) Pray over the sermon and plead with God. I pray for a few things each week: clarity, conviction, that it hits the heart, that it teaches the mind, that the sermon proclaims the glories Christ and Him crucified, that it will reform hearts, that it will convict of sin, and provoke God's people to good works.*
>
> *2) Ruthlessly go through and edit it down. Not everything that can be said needs to be said. A clear sermon is better than a long sermon. Some of the best sermon writing is done at this point. Don't be afraid to use a red pen or the delete button.*

**This phase exists to address Bryan's known weakness: sermons run too long. The LLM's role here is manuscript review — point out density, sprawl, and places to cut, with reasoning. Bryan still does the cutting.**
